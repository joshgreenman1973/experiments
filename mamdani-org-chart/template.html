How to read it

Columns are the officials who report to the mayor. Boxes beneath each one are the agencies and offices in that portfolio. Numbers are a filing sequence, not a rank. A box marked with a triangle opens into that department's own chart.

What is not here

  • Deputy and assistant commissioners, except where the Green Book publishes them. {{MAPPED}} of the boxes open into a department chart; the rest have none published.
  • Offices the city's agency file records with no reporting line, and organizations it lists that are not part of city government.
  • Rank-and-file civil servants, who are not political appointees.
  • Officials the mayor does not appoint, such as the chancellor of the City University of New York.

Where the data comes from

The top layer, the {{TOTAL}} appointees, comes from a running tracker of every appointment and departure since Jan. 1, 2026, sourced to mayor's office press releases, agency leadership pages, City & State New York, The City, Gothamist, Crain's New York Business, Streetsblog and amNewYork.

Reporting lines and the officers of offices with no announcement come from the city's agency file, snapshotted into this repo so a change in it shows up as a reviewable diff. Salaries and the last personnel action shown against a name come from the City Record, by way of the same directory feed.

The department layer is fetched at page load from the Greener Book, which rebuilds from the city's own Green Book, the agency governance file and the City Record every four hours. That layer therefore updates without anyone rebuilding this page.

Reporting lines

The city publishes {{CITYLINES}} of them, in the Office of Technology and Innovation's agency and governance file. Open any box and it shows where that file puts the office, and says so plainly when this chart groups it somewhere else on the strength of an announcement. Offices with no published line sit in their own column rather than being assigned to a guess.

That file is updated annually, so a few of its lines still name posts from the last administration. Those are mapped to the post that absorbed the work, and the ones with no successor are left out rather than guessed at. {{EXTRAS}} offices it lists, which no announcement covered, are carried at the foot of the column the file assigns them to.

Where the Green Book and the governance file name different people as an agency head, both are shown with the reason one is preferred.

Everything substantive here, the concept, the judgment about who counts as an appointee and the editorial decisions throughout, were handled by the author, a human. The coding and the data assembly were primarily in the hands of Anthropic's Claude and Claude Code, under close direction, with quality controls insisted on throughout: every box links to a named press release or news report, department charts are shown only where the city publishes one, and names that could not be verified were left out rather than guessed. The risks of using AI remain. If you spot a red flag, an error or an omission, please say so.
