Social Security Administration, names on card applications 1880 to 2024

The baby name horserace

Of the 50 most common boys’ names in 1880, 12 were still in the top 50 in 2024. For girls, 5 were.

Since 1880, 206 boys’ names and 314 girls’ names have spent at least one year in the top 50. Five boys’ names never left it: John, James, William, Joseph and David. Among girls, only Elizabeth has stayed the whole way.

The field has also spread out. In 1880 the top 50 names went to 70 percent of boys and 58 percent of girls. In 2024 they went to 25 percent and 21 percent, while the number of girls’ names in use grew from 942 to 17,661. The No. 1 girls’ name, Mary, went to 7.8 percent of girls in 1880; Olivia went to 0.9 percent in 2024. How this was made

The data

The counts are the Social Security Administration’s national baby names file: for every year since 1880, each first name given to at least five babies of one sex, with the number of Social Security card applications that carried it. The agency’s site refuses automated downloads, so the yearly files were taken from a copy on GitHub (1880 through 2024) and spot-checked against the agency’s published figures: 7,065 Marys in 1880, and Liam (22,164) and Olivia (14,718) at the top in 2024.

The race starts in 1880 because that is where Social Security’s records start. There is no national count of baby names before then.

How ranks were calculated

Within each year and sex, names are ranked by count, highest first. Ties are broken alphabetically, which can put a name one place off the agency’s own lists in rare ties near the bottom of the top 50. Spellings count separately, so Catherine and Katherine are different names, as they are in the source.

The share next to each name (and in each name’s description) divides the name’s count by the total of all names listed for that sex that year. Names given to fewer than five babies are not in the file, so the true share is slightly lower.

Line colour groups names by the first year they reached the top 50. Names already there in 1880 get their own colour because the data cannot say when they arrived.

The figures at the top

  • 12 boys’ names and 5 girls’ names were in the top 50 in both 1880 and 2024. Boys: Benjamin, Daniel, David, Henry, Jacob, James, John, Joseph, Michael, Samuel, Thomas and William. Girls: Elizabeth, Ella, Emma, Grace and Lucy.
  • 206 boys’ names and 314 girls’ names ranked 50th or better in at least one year from 1880 to 2024.
  • Top-50 coverage: the counts of that year’s 50 highest-ranked names added up, divided by all names listed for that sex. 1880: 70.3 percent of boys, 58.4 percent of girls. 2024: 25.1 percent of boys, 21.1 percent of girls. Both hit their lowest point in 2023, at 25.05 and 21.13 percent.
  • Names in use is the number of distinct names in the file for that sex and year, so it counts only names given to five or more babies. Girls: 942 in 1880, 17,661 in 2024. Boys: 1,058 and 14,243. The early counts are also held down by the undercount of early births described below.
  • No. 1 shares: Mary, 7,065 of 90,994 girls listed in 1880 (7.8 percent); Olivia, 14,718 of 1,613,188 in 2024 (0.9 percent).
  • John, James, William, Joseph and David ranked in the boys’ top 50 in all 145 years. Elizabeth is the only girls’ name that did.

Limits

  • Social Security cards became common only after 1937. Most records for people born earlier come from applications filed years later, so the early years undercount births and lean toward people who lived long enough to apply.
  • The file records sex as listed on the application, female or male.
  • 2024 is the latest year in this copy of the data.
