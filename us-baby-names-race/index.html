Social Security Administration, names on card applications 1880 to 2025

The baby name horserace

Of the 50 most common boys’ names in 1880, 13 were still in the top 50 in 2025. For girls, 5 were.

Since 1880, 209 boys’ names and 316 girls’ names have spent at least one year in the top 50. Five boys’ names never left it: John, James, William, Joseph and David. Among girls, only Elizabeth has stayed the whole way.

The field has also spread out. In 1880 the top 50 names went to 70 percent of boys and 58 percent of girls. In 2025 they went to 25 percent and 21 percent, while the number of girls’ names in use grew from 942 to 17,297. The No. 1 girls’ name, Mary, went to 7.8 percent of girls in 1880; Olivia went to 0.8 percent in 2025. Each name in the race shows its share, and the strip under each chart tracks how much of the whole field the top 50 covered. How this was made

The data

The counts are the Social Security Administration’s national baby names file: for every year since 1880, each first name given to at least five babies of one sex, with the number of Social Security card applications that carried it. The agency’s site refuses automated downloads, so the file was downloaded by hand on Nov. 23, 2026. It is the agency’s current release, covering 1880 through 2025. Each release revises earlier years slightly as late applications come in (Linda’s 1950 count went from 80,431 in the previous release to 80,433 in this one), so figures here can differ by a few births from older copies. At the top in 2025: Olivia (13,544) and Liam (20,818).

The race starts in 1880 because that is where Social Security’s records start. There is no national count of baby names before then.

States

The state races use Social Security’s separate state file (namesbystate.zip, downloaded by hand Nov. 23, 2026), which counts names by the state where the card application was filed, from 1910 through 2025. Ranks, shares and colours are calculated the same way as the national race, within each state.

The state file also leaves out any name given to fewer than five babies in that state and year, and that cutoff bites hard in small states. In Wyoming in 2025, the 50th-ranked girls’ name went to 7 babies; in 1910 only 23 girls’ names cleared the bar there. When a state’s 50th-ranked name in 2025 went to fewer than 25 babies, or some years list fewer than 50 names, the page says so above the charts. In those states the lower ranks rest on a few births and many ties, and the share in the top 50 runs high because the uncounted names are left out of the total.

How ranks were calculated

Within each year and sex, names are ranked by count, highest first. Ties are broken alphabetically, which can put a name one place off the agency’s own lists in rare ties near the bottom of the top 50. Spellings count separately, so Catherine and Katherine are different names, as they are in the source.

The share next to each name (and in each name’s description) divides the name’s count by the total of all names listed for that sex that year. Names given to fewer than five babies are not in the file, so the true share is slightly lower.

When a name is highlighted, the chart labels it as far as the race has run: gold bars along the top mark its years at No. 1, a ring marks the year it went to the largest share of babies, and the bottom edge shows when it entered and left the top 50 (for names with more than three stretches in the top 50, only the first entry and the last exit). Beside the long-tail strip, “names (5+)” is the number of names given to at least five babies that year.

Line colour groups names by the first year they reached the top 50. Names already there in 1880 get their own colour because the data cannot say when they arrived.

The figures at the top

  • 13 boys’ names and 5 girls’ names were in the top 50 in both 1880 and 2025. Boys: Benjamin, Charles, Daniel, David, Henry, Jacob, James, John, Joseph, Michael, Samuel, Thomas and William. Girls: Elizabeth, Ella, Emma, Grace and Lucy.
  • 209 boys’ names and 316 girls’ names ranked 50th or better in at least one year from 1880 to 2025.
  • Top-50 coverage: the counts of that year’s 50 highest-ranked names added up, divided by all names listed for that sex. 1880: 70.3 percent of boys, 58.4 percent of girls. 2025: 25.0 percent of boys, 21.4 percent of girls. The low point for boys is 2025 (25.05 percent); for girls it was 2023 (21.13 percent).
  • Names in use is the number of distinct names in the file for that sex and year, so it counts only names given to five or more babies. Girls: 942 in 1880, 17,297 in 2025. Boys: 1,058 and 13,930. The early counts are also held down by the undercount of early births described below.
  • No. 1 shares: Mary, 7,065 of 90,994 girls listed in 1880 (7.8 percent); Olivia, 13,544 of 1,607,267 in 2025 (0.8 percent).
  • John, James, William, Joseph and David ranked in the boys’ top 50 in all 146 years. Elizabeth is the only girls’ name that did.

Limits

  • Social Security cards became common only after 1937. Most records for people born earlier come from applications filed years later, so the early years undercount births and lean toward people who lived long enough to apply.
  • The file records sex as listed on the application, female or male.
  • 2025 is the latest year Social Security has published.
