Every number on this page comes from a fixed panel of real New York businesses or a published public series, measured the same way every month — so what rises and falls here is the same thing New Yorkers are actually paying.

Four rules, applied to every item:
  • Fixed panel. The same stores, stations, theaters, or data series each month — no dropping out when prices jump.
  • Same spec. Same size, same cut, same day of week, same time of day, cash/posted price, tax excluded.
  • Same math. A simple arithmetic mean of the panel. No weighting, no seasonal smoothing, no adjustments.
  • Work shown. Panel composition, the individual prices, and the calculation are all in the open below and on GitHub.

Full methodology — how every line is built

Three kinds of series, three kinds of math

  • Fixed-panel surveys (6 items): pizza, bagel, coffee, family dinner, movie ticket, Uber benchmark. We collect the posted/cash price from the same named businesses on the same day each month and take a straight arithmetic mean.
  • Published public series (3 items): gas (AAA), rent (StreetEasy), groceries (BLS CPI). We record the published value on a fixed date each month. No transformation.
  • Rate-card construction (1 item): ConEd electricity. We rebuild a 300-kWh Manhattan residential bill from ConEd's posted monthly tariff components.

What this doesn't measure

Wages, income-adjusted burden, tip inflation, shrinkflation (smaller slices at the same price), rent-stabilized contracts (the rent index covers listings only), or the full cost of owning a car. It's a directional gauge of ten prices, not a cost-of-living index.

Why these ten?

Each one is either touched by almost everyone (gas, groceries, electricity, rent) or is a familiar, easy-to-price New York ritual (slice, bagel, coffee, movie, family dinner, ride to Midtown). Together they span food, housing, utilities, transport, and culture — enough surface area to see if a month is broadly softening or broadly tightening.

Panel turnover

If a panel business closes, we note it, freeze that slot at its last observed price for the month, and pick a replacement of similar neighborhood and tier for the next month — with both the outgoing and incoming entries flagged in the changelog on GitHub. Prices never silently shift because we swapped the sample.

Reproducibility

Every panel list, every monthly reading, and the averaging spreadsheet live on GitHub. If a number on this page looks wrong, the raw data is a click away.
