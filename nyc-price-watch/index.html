Every dollar figure on this page was pulled from a public source or posted tariff this week. Anywhere I couldn't verify a current number, the card is flagged TBD — not a guess — so the trend line, when it forms, is built only from real observations.

Four rules, applied to every item:
  • Real or nothing. Every printed number ties back to a named public source with a retrieval date. No guesses. TBD where not yet observed.
  • Same spec each month. Same tariff series, same panel of businesses, same day and time of collection.
  • Same math. Published series are recorded as-is. Panel items are the arithmetic mean of the panel — no weighting, no smoothing.
  • Work shown. Panel composition, the individual prices, and the calculation all live in readings.json in the repo.

Full methodology

Three kinds of series, three kinds of math

  • Published tariffs (7 items): subway, gas, toll, Citi Bike, water, ConEd, Broadway, taxi — recorded straight from the source. No transformation except ConEd, where we rebuild a 300 kWh bill from the posted rate schedule.
  • Public indices (2 items): StreetEasy citywide median, BLS NY-Newark-JC food-at-home CPI.
  • Fixed-panel surveys (5 items): pizza slice, bagel, coffee, Uber benchmark, family dinner. Arithmetic mean of a named panel, same spec every month.

How the monthly log works

Every reading is appended to readings.json in this project's repo — one entry per item per month, with value, date, source, and any notes. After three consecutive months of real readings, sparklines and percentage-change badges turn on for each card. Until then the cards show a single verified value or an explicit TBD.

What this doesn't measure

Wages, income-adjusted burden, tip inflation, shrinkflation, rent-stabilized contracts, or the full cost of owning a car. It's a directional gauge of ten (soon fifteen) prices, not a cost-of-living index.

Reproducibility

Every panel list, every monthly reading, and every source URL live in readings.json on GitHub. If a number on this page looks wrong, the raw data is a click away.
