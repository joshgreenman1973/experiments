New York tax levers

 

Describe your household, then switch on any of the state and city income tax changes now being discussed in Albany and at City Hall. The page shows what your family would owe New York State and New York City for 2026, and what each change would add or save. The rules, rates and credits come from PolicyEngine US, the open-source tax and benefit model built by PolicyEngine, translated to run in your browser and checked against it. The levers include every New York bill that PolicyEngine has built into its model, plus the other proposals we found under discussion. It covers wage earners who take the standard deduction and live in the five boroughs.

The levers

Each one starts at current law. Turn it on, then adjust the numbers. Changes to the city's income tax also need a vote in Albany, because the state sets the city's rates. Each lever shows its own effect; when several are on, they can overlap, so the total above won't always equal their sum.

Credit. Every tax rule, rate, threshold and credit formula on this page comes from PolicyEngine US (version 2.145.7), built by PolicyEngine. This page translates the parts that apply to New York wage earners into JavaScript so the levers respond instantly. It was run side by side with PolicyEngine on 1,620 made-up households under 19 scenarios, including each of PolicyEngine's New York bill reforms, and all 430,920 values agreed to within a dollar, apart from rounding of a few dollars at incomes in the tens of millions. A few pieces of the levers go beyond what PolicyEngine models and were checked by hand against the bills; each lever says which. Any mistakes here are this page's, not PolicyEngine's.
