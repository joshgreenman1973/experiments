What Does Dinner
Actually Cost?

We checked menus at 250+ neighborhood restaurants across all five boroughs to find out what a family of four actually pays for a weeknight dinner in New York City — no tablecloths, no tasting menus, just the places where people eat.

Restaurants were drawn from every NYC neighborhood — weighted toward everyday, family-friendly spots with at least 25 Google reviews, and validated via Google Places to confirm each was open, correctly located, and not permanently closed. For each one, we selected the most ordered-looking items on the menu: the signature entrée or pie, the plainest kid-friendly option, and a standard shared side or appetizer — so every bill reflects what a family would actually order, not the cheapest or priciest thing available.

Two adult entrées (or a shared pie/dishes where that's how families order), two kid portions, one shared side, two sodas (kids get water). Tax (8.875%) and 18% tip included. All prices verified from in-restaurant menus, not delivery platforms.

Sources & Methodology

Where the prices come from

Every price on this page comes from the restaurant's own menu — either the printed menu posted in-store or the menu on the restaurant's official website. We do not use third-party delivery platforms (Grubhub, DoorDash, Uber Eats, Seamless), where menu prices are routinely marked up 15–40% above dine-in pricing.

How restaurants were selected

  • 250+ restaurants across all five NYC boroughs, weighted toward neighborhood spots families actually use — not tasting-menu destinations or tourist traps.
  • Every restaurant is validated against the Google Places API (New): must exist, must be currently open, must have at least 25 Google reviews, and the stored location must match Google's coordinates within 500 meters.
  • Restaurants flagged as permanently closed by Google — or that go dark on three consecutive monthly checks — are removed from averages.

What counts as "a family dinner"

The meal structure is standardized so every restaurant is comparable. We pick actual menu items from each restaurant (shown in the receipt when you click a dot on the map).

  • Standard (most restaurants): 2 adult entrées + 2 kid-sized portions + 1 shared appetizer or side + 2 sodas (kids drink water).
  • Pizza shops: 1 large cheese pie + 1 side (knots, garlic bread, etc.) + 2 sodas.
  • Chinese takeout-style: 2 shared entrées + white rice + 1 appetizer + 2 sodas.
  • Excluded: alcohol, dessert, delivery fees, service charges beyond tip.

Tax and tip

NYC sales tax (8.875%) is applied to the food subtotal. An 18% tip is added on the pre-tax subtotal — the standard etiquette benchmark. All "total bill" figures on this page are fully loaded (food + tax + tip).

Two parts: a cross-section and a tracked panel

This project is two things, and we keep them separate on purpose:

  • The cross-section (the map): a one-time snapshot of 360+ restaurants across all five boroughs, compiled in spring 2026. It answers "what does a family dinner cost around the city right now?" It is a dated snapshot, not a live feed — the prices on the map are not re-checked every month.
  • The tracked panel (the trend): a smaller set of 22 restaurants whose menus are reliably readable online. For each one we pinned the exact menu line items (the specific dishes, copied verbatim) at a baseline. Each month we re-fetch the menu and re-price those same lines — and only those. If a pinned line is gone or unreadable, we carry its last price forward and flag it rather than substituting a different dish. This is what makes the month-over-month trend trustworthy: we are always comparing the same bill.

Why a panel instead of tracking all 360? Most restaurant menus today are rendered by JavaScript or sit behind ordering widgets, so a simple fetch can't read their prices — and an AI that free-picks a "similar" item each month produces fake price swings, not real ones. We would rather track 22 restaurants honestly than 360 unreliably. Soft drinks are standardized at $2.50 citywide (menus rarely list soda prices), so drinks add no false month-to-month movement. The panel can grow as we hand-pin more menus. Re-checking is run manually, not on a cron — there is no always-on automated fetcher.

Known limitations

  • Menu prices change between monthly checks — any given day's real bill may differ by a few dollars.
  • "Kid-sized portion" is our judgment call when a restaurant doesn't have a formal kids' menu; we aim for a small entrée or half-size dish.
  • BYOB, happy-hour pricing, and lunch specials are ignored — this is weeknight dinner pricing.
  • Small counter-service or mostly-takeout spots may not offer a true sit-down experience; the total reflects the cost of the food, not the ambiance.
