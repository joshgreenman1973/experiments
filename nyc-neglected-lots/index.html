The lots nobody fixes

Of the city's 24,461 vacant lots, 737 drew records of neglect from two or more agencies between September 2024 and August 2026, and 670 of those are privately owned.

Every vacant lot in New York City, every building the city has put on a distress list and every park, scored by how many separate agencies recorded neglect there in the last 24 months: neighbors through 311, sanitation and health inspectors, the housing, buildings and finance departments, and the Parks Department's own inspectors. Of 24,461 vacant lots, 2,701 have at least one record and 92 have three or more. The map also carries 2,460 distressed buildings and 269 parks flagged by two or more sources. Underneath sits the sanitation department's lot-cleaning file from 2010 to 2021: 2,042 lots it cleaned then are still vacant.

And please read this AI caution. The concept, the choice of sources and the layout are the author's (a human). Quality control done on this project: every source count was re-queried directly against the New York City Open Data API and compared with the built files; the join keys were spot-checked by hand against the city's property records; the sanitation history layer was checked by reading raw inspector notes for sampled lots.
