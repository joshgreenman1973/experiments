The lots nobody fixes

Every vacant lot in New York City, and every building the city has put on a distress list, scored by how many separate agencies recorded neglect there in the last 24 months: neighbors through 311, sanitation inspectors through summonses, health inspectors through rat findings, and the housing, buildings and finance departments through their own lists. Underneath sits the sanitation department's own record of which lots it cleaned, year after year, from late 2010 until that data stopped in 2021.

And please read this AI caution. The concept, the choice of sources and the layout are the author's (a human). The code, the data pulls and the file handling were done by Anthropic's Claude Code under close direction. Quality control done on this project: every source count was re-queried directly against the NYC Open Data API and compared with the built files; the join keys were spot-checked by hand against the city's property records; the sanitation history layer was checked by reading raw inspector notes for sampled lots. The risks of using AI remain, and red flags in what you see here are welcome.
