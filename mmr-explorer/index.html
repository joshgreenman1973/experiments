Report card

The city charter makes New York City grade its own agencies every September. The Mayor's Management Report is that grade — thousands of counts, rates, response times and backlogs, published as a stack of PDFs and a 768,409-row spreadsheet almost nobody opens. This reads the whole thing at once: what moved the way the city says it should, what moved against it, what swung further than it ever has, and what quietly stopped being counted.

Read the fine print before you quote anything. Every figure here is the city's own, carried through without correction, re-basing or modelling. Indicator titles and their descriptions disagree often enough that the description is the only reliable statement of what is being counted — it is shown with every chart. Percentages, counts, dollars and response times are not comparable to one another, and a large percentage change off a small base is usually a change in definition rather than a change on the ground. Where a published figure cannot be what its label says, it is flagged and left exactly as filed.
