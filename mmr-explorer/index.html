Report card

The city charter makes New York City grade its own agencies every September. The Mayor's Management Report is that grade — thousands of counts, rates, response times and backlogs, published as a stack of PDFs and a 768,409-row spreadsheet almost nobody opens. This reads the whole thing at once: what moved the way the city says it should, what moved against it, what swung further than it ever has, and what quietly stopped being counted.

Where the figures come from

Historical values come from NYC Open Data. For matched series, the fiscal 2026 report supplies the completed year and its available revised history. Original values that differ are retained in the indicator detail. Spending and staffing use final prior-year PMMR actuals where available.

What the comparisons mean

The scoreboard follows the city's stated direction for the compared year. Exact equality is unchanged. Missing data, zero baselines, flagged figures and documented definition breaks can prevent scoring. Counts of indicators are not an overall grade for an agency.

What remains uncertain

Unmatched PDF rows are extraction gaps, not proof of missing publication. School-year and calendar-year indicators retain their own periods. Most chapter footnotes have not been structured into the data, so check definitions before quoting long trends.

What was checked

The September 18 audit checked 49,219 historical numeric cells against June source rows and reproduced all 238 original ratio calculations. Independent PDF extraction checked most matched numerical rows, with ambiguous cases identified separately.

What was corrected

The audit found reversed direction labels, a zero-threshold scoring bug, outdated spending actuals, misleading ratio descriptions, and gaps counted as consecutive years. The build and interface now carry those fixes and regression checks. Five reviewed DOB revisions restore figures that the automatic matching had omitted.

Read the method, checks and limitations, or inspect the source and tests.

Check definitions before quoting a finding. An indicator count is not an agency grade. Large changes can reflect real events, revisions or changed definitions. The ratios show matching subsets and totals over five reviewed years; each states its interpretation and limits. Hover or focus an indicator for the city's description, and open it for figures and sources.
