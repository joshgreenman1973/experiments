# Women & Guns in America — Data & Sources

**Purpose.** This is the editable companion to `index.html`. It documents every figure shown in
the story — the hero stats, the cards, the bar charts, the waffle chart, the comparison panels and
the pull-quotes — together with the exact dataset and source behind it, the supporting language from
that source, and any complexities or open questions a careful reader should weigh.

**How to read it.** Each entry gives the figure as published, the source (dataset, organization,
year, link), what the source actually says, and — where it matters — a *Complexities & questions*
note. Several recurring caveats apply throughout and are collected at the end.

**Maintainer note.** When a number changes in `index.html`, change it here too. Keep the two in sync.

## Hero stats

**42M women own guns.**
Source: [Pew Research Center, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/24/key-facts-about-americans-and-guns/) — "40% of men say they own a gun, versus 25% of women."
*Complexities & questions:* Pew states the **25%** rate, not the **42 million** count. The count is a
derivation: 25% × the U.S. adult female population (~169 million, Census) ≈ 42 million. It is labeled
in the story as an approximate, derived estimate. If the population base is updated, recompute the count.

**6,500+ women killed by guns each year.**
Source: [Giffords Law Center, *The Devastating Toll of Gun Violence on American Women and Girls*](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/report/the-devastating-toll-of-gun-violence-on-american-women-and-girls/) — "More than 6,500 women die from gun violence every year."
*Complexities & questions:* Giffords' phrasing is "die from gun violence," which **includes suicides**
(the majority of these deaths). It is not a homicide-only figure.

**5x intimate-partner homicide risk with an abuser's gun access.**
Source: [Campbell et al., *American Journal of Public Health*, 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1447915/) — adjusted odds ratio 5.44 (95% CI 2.89–10.22).
*Complexities & questions:* This is an **odds ratio** from a case-control study of intimate-partner
femicide, best read as "roughly five times higher odds," not a literal causal multiplier (see the
odds-ratio note at the end).

## Chapter 1 — Ownership

### Three ownership cards

**25% of U.S. women personally own a firearm (~42 million).**
Source: [Pew Research Center, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/24/key-facts-about-americans-and-guns/). See hero note on the derived 42M count.

**3.5M women became new gun owners, January 2019–April 2021.**
Source: 2021 National Firearms Survey (Miller & Azrael, Harvard); primary publication [*Annals of Internal Medicine*, 2021 (PMC8697522)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8697522/).
*Complexities & questions:* A survey-based estimate, not an administrative count (there is no national
registry of gun owners). The same survey underlies the ~50% figure below.

**~50% of new gun owners in 2019–2021 were women (50% in 2019; 47% in 2020–21).**
Source: [*Annals of Internal Medicine*, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8697522/) — "approximately half of all new gun owners were female (50% in 2019 and 47% in 2020 to 2021)."

### Black-women buyers (narrative + pull-quote)

**Nearly 87% of firearm retailers reported increased sales to Black women in 2021.**
Source: [NSSF, 2021](https://www.nssf.org/articles/firearm-industry-celebrates-our-black-community-and-family/) — "Nearly 87 percent of retailers reported an increase of African American women purchasing firearms in 2021."
*Complexities & questions:* This is a **retailer-survey** figure (share of retailers reporting an
increase), not a sales volume. NSSF is the firearm industry's trade association — attribute by name.

**About 1 in 5 (20–21%) of new gun owners in 2019–2021 were Black.**
Source: [*Annals of Internal Medicine*, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8697522/) — "20% were Black (21% in 2019 and in 2020–2021)."
*Complexities & questions:* This describes **all new gun owners** (men and women), not Black women's
share of new women owners. Stated that way in the story.

**~72% of gun owners (and ~71% of women owners) cite protection as a major reason.**
Sources: overall, [Pew, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2023/08/16/for-most-u-s-gun-owners-protection-is-the-main-reason-they-own-a-gun/) — "72% of U.S. gun owners say protection is a major reason"; women-specific, [Pew, 2017](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2017/06/29/how-male-and-female-gun-owners-in-the-u-s-compare/) — women = 71% major reason.
*Complexities & questions:* The two figures come from **different Pew surveys/years** (2023 overall,
2017 women-specific). They are close but not directly comparable year-over-year.

## Chapter 2 — Harms

### Waffle chart — "For every 100 women who die by a gun"

Suicide **52%** · Homicide **43%** · Other/accidental **5%**.
Source: [Giffords Law Center](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/report/the-devastating-toll-of-gun-violence-on-american-women-and-girls/), drawing on CDC WONDER female firearm deaths by intent, 2018–2023.
*Complexities & questions:* The split comes from **Giffords / CDC WONDER**, not the general CDC
firearm-facts page (which has no sex-specific intent breakdown). These shares are a **2018–2023
average and drift year to year**; the "other/accidental" slice rounds 5% and the three sum to 100.
The 100-cell waffle is a proportional representation, not 100 specific deaths.

### Three harms cards

**21x — American women are 21 times more likely to die from gun violence than women in other high-income countries.**
Source: [Giffords Law Center](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/report/the-devastating-toll-of-gun-violence-on-american-women-and-girls/), based on Grinshteyn & Hemenway cross-national research.
*Complexities & questions:* **The most methodology-sensitive figure in the story.** It depends on the
comparison-country set, the data year, and whether "gun violence" means all gun deaths or homicide
only (Giffords uses "die from gun violence," which includes suicide). A related cross-national claim
was previously rated "Half True" by [PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2016/mar/14/everytown-gun-safety/). The story attributes it to Giffords and frames it as their figure, not a settled fact.

**~88% of female homicide victims (relationship known) were killed by someone they knew.**
Source: [Violence Policy Center, *When Men Murder Women*, 2024](https://vpc.org/when-men-murder-women/) (2022 data) — 87.5%.
*Complexities & questions:* VPC's universe is specifically **females killed by males in
single-victim/single-offender incidents** where the relationship could be identified. "~88%" rounds
87.5%.

**5.5x — Black women's gun homicide rate vs. white women (6.1 vs. 1.1 per 100,000).**
Source: [Giffords Law Center](https://giffords.org/lawcenter/report/the-devastating-toll-of-gun-violence-on-american-women-and-girls/).
*Complexities & questions:* The rates (6.1 and 1.1 per 100,000) are the source figures; the **5.5x
multiplier is computed** from them (6.1 ÷ 1.1).

## Chapter 3 — Domestic Violence

### Three DV cards

**5x — an abuser's gun access is associated with roughly five times higher odds of intimate-partner homicide.**
Source: [Campbell et al., 2003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1447915/) — adjusted OR 5.44. (See hero note; odds ratio.)

**70+ women shot and killed by an intimate partner every month.**
Source: [Everytown Research](https://everytownresearch.org/report/guns-and-violence-against-women/) — "an average of more than 70 women are shot and killed by an intimate partner."
*Complexities & questions:* The 70-figure icon array below the cards is a proportional illustration
(70 person glyphs), not 70 named individuals.

**~6M women report having had a gun used on them by an intimate partner.**
Source: [Everytown Research](https://everytownresearch.org/report/guns-and-violence-against-women/), drawing on CDC's National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey (NISVS) — "Nearly 6 million women reported having a gun used on them by an intimate partner."
*Complexities & questions:* "Used on them" spans **shot, shot at, or threatened** — broader than
"threatened" alone. It is a lifetime self-report from a national survey.

| Figure | Source |

| 77% of intimate-partner firearm-homicide victims are women | [Everytown](https://everytownresearch.org/report/guns-and-violence-against-women/) — "77 percent… are women" |

*Complexities & questions:* These figures come from **three different organizations** measuring

source line in the story credits each. Everytown and VPC are advocacy-aligned — attribute by name.

### Comparison panel — mass shootings & murder-suicides

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| 54% of mass shootings (2009–2016) involved domestic or family violence | [Everytown](https://www.everytown.org/press/women-and-children-in-the-crosshairs-new-analysis-of-mass-shootings-in-america-reveals-54-percent-involved-domestic-violence-and-25-percent-of-fatalities-were-children/) |
| 59–68% of mass shootings (2014–2019) were DV-related or by a perpetrator with DV history | [Geller et al., *Injury Epidemiology*, 2021 (PMC8165999)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40621-021-00330-0) — 59.1% DV-related; 68.2% incl. DV history |
| Victims of DV-related mass shootings less likely to survive (case-fatality ~84% vs ~63%) | [Geller et al., 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40621-021-00330-0) — 83.7% vs 63.1% |
| ~91% of murder-suicide perpetrators male; ~95% of intimate-partner murder-suicide victims female | [VPC, *American Roulette*, 2023](https://vpc.org/studies/amroul2023.pdf) — "91 percent… males"; "95 percent were females killed by their intimate partners" |
| 40%+ of those killed in DV-related mass shootings are children | [Everytown](https://www.everytown.org/press/women-and-children-in-the-crosshairs-new-analysis-of-mass-shootings-in-america-reveals-54-percent-involved-domestic-violence-and-25-percent-of-fatalities-were-children/) |

different time windows (Everytown 2009–2016; Geller 2014–2019; VPC's murder-suicide study is a

total victims but a *higher share of victims who die*. VPC's *American Roulette* covers a specific
six-month period, so its percentages reflect that sample.

## Chapter 4 — Suicide

### Two suicide cards

**Firearms have been the leading method of suicide for women since 2020.**
Source: [CDC NCHS Data Brief No. 509](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db509.htm) — "firearm-related suicide has been the leading means of suicide [for females] since 2020."

### Pull-quote

**For every justifiable firearm homicide by a civilian, there are roughly 34 criminal gun homicides, dozens of gun suicides and several accidental gun deaths.**

Women **64%** · Men **51%**.
Source: [Pew Research Center, 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/07/24/key-facts-about-americans-and-guns/) — "A majority of women (64%)… compared with 51% of men."

**RAND: moderate evidence that DV-restraining-order gun bans reduce firearm and total intimate-partner homicides.**

## Recurring caveats (apply throughout)

1. **Odds ratios are associations, not proven causal multipliers.** Several headline figures — the
   5x abuser-gun-access risk (Campbell), the ~3x household-gun suicide risk (Anglemyer), the ~7x and
   ~2x handgun-household risks (Stanford) — are odds ratios from observational studies. "Roughly five
   times," "triples," "seven times," "doubles" are fair plain-language renderings of those odds, but
   they describe correlations, not experiments.

2. **The "21x" international comparison is methodology-sensitive.** It varies with the comparison set,
   the data year, and whether it counts all gun deaths or homicides only. Attribute to Giffords /
   Grinshteyn-Hemenway; do not present as settled.

3. **Advocacy and trade sources are attributed by name.** Everytown, Giffords, the Violence Policy
   Center, the Crime Prevention Research Center and the NSSF all have a point of view. Their numbers
   are reported against their stated methods, but the story names them so readers can weigh the source.

4. **Survey vs. administrative data.** Ownership and victimization figures (Pew, Gallup, the Harvard
   National Firearms Survey, NISVS, NCVS) are survey estimates with sampling error, not registries.
   Self-reported and self-published figures (e.g. A Girl & A Gun) carry the most uncertainty.

5. **Mixed vintages and definitions.** Some charts and panels combine figures from different years,
   different "mass shooting" definitions, or different victim universes. Where that happens, the story
   carries an on-page note (most visibly the demographic ownership chart and the suicide-rate chart).
