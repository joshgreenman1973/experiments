25 ways the city has changed since 9/11

Twenty-five measures of New York City as it was in the month of the attacks and as it is now, drawn from the Census Bureau, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the state and city comptrollers, the Port Authority, the MTA, the NYPD, the CDC and the Alliance for Downtown New York. Blue is 2001 or the nearest year on record. Orange is the latest figure. The year of each is printed under it.

How to read this

Each pair compares the figure closest to September 2001 with the most recent one published. Where the 2001 figure comes from the 2000 census or the 2002 survey year, that year is printed under the number. Percent changes are computed from the values shown and are not adjusted for inflation unless the row says so.

Every number was required to come from a source page fetched during research, with a verbatim quote recorded. The quotes and links are on the methodology and sources page.

About

Built by Josh Greenman, September 2026. Data as of the dates shown. Corrections: josh.greenman@gmail.com.

The concept, the choice of measures and the layout were the author's (a human). The research, coding and file handling were done by Anthropic's Claude Code under close direction. Quality controls on this project: every figure had to come from a source page fetched during research, with a verbatim quote recorded next to it; figures that could not be fetched were dropped rather than estimated; the percent changes were recomputed from the published values by script; and the author reviewed each pairing against its source. Risks of AI error remain. If you spot a wrong number, a stale figure or a better source, the author would like to hear it.
