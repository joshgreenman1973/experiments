Who represents New York City

Phone numbers, emails and office addresses for the 175 people elected to represent the five boroughs in Washington, Albany and City Hall, from the two U.S. senators down to the 51 City Council members. For 115 of those offices the page also names staff members and gives a direct line or address that the office has published for them: 386 people in all, 350 with an email address of their own and 87 with a phone number of their own. Shared inboxes such as press@ are shown but not counted.

170 of the 175 offices have a named chief of staff, press secretary or communications director. Where an office does not publish one, the name comes from official payroll records of the Assembly, State Senate and U.S. House (155 names) or from the news coverage that most recently identified who speaks for the official (54 names), each linked to its source.

24 people won seats on Nov. 3, 2026 that they do not hold yet, and one more leads a race too close to treat as settled. They appear beside the officials they are set to replace in January.

This page covers elected offices. Deputy mayors, commissioners and other appointed officials are in the Mamdani administration org chart.

Where this comes from

  • Congress: the House Clerk’s member roster, the Senate’s contact directory and each member’s own office page.
  • Albany: each senator’s contact page on nysenate.gov and the Assembly’s member directory.
  • City Council: the Council’s district roster and each member’s page.
  • Statewide, citywide and borough offices: each office’s own contact page, linked in its row.
  • Direct lines for senior staff in city offices: the city’s Green Book, by way of the Greener Book. The Green Book can lag after staff changes.
  • Chiefs of staff and communications staff for House members: the House’s Statement of Disbursements for April to June 2026, which lists each office’s staff by name and title.
  • Chiefs of staff and communications staff for state legislators: the Assembly’s Expenditure Report (staff on each member’s payroll through March 11, 2026) and the Senate’s Payroll Report for the period ending Nov. 4, 2026. They give a name and job title, no contact. The Assembly record is eight months old.
  • Other named staff: staff pages, press releases and newsletters the offices published. For Congress, also the invitee lists attached to House Clerk travel filings, the Senate Radio-TV Gallery’s press secretary list and an archive of the offices’ own emailed releases. Each entry links to the page it was read from.

The rule on emails and phone numbers

An address or number appears here only if a page published it. None was built from a naming pattern, even where the pattern is plain.

Staff contacts were gathered by searching the web, which can return an address that only looks right. So each one was checked a second way: the cited page was downloaded again and searched for the same characters. Contacts that failed that check were left out. 356 researched staff contacts passed it.

Entries marked “Named in” come from news coverage, not from the office. Each article was downloaded and checked for the person’s name. They carry no contact unless the article prints one, and a spokesperson quoted last month may have moved on.

Members of Congress, the governor and the mayor publish no personal email address. Their rows give the web form and the people around them.

Press secretaries change jobs often. Check the date beside a staff entry before relying on it.

Election results

Winners come from the New York City Board of Elections’ unofficial election night results, last updated 2026-11-04 00:42:08 Eastern with about 97% of scanners reporting. They are not certified, and absentee and affidavit ballots were still to be counted.

For four districts that cross the city line (the 3rd and 16th Congressional, the 34th and 36th State Senate) the city count covers only the city portion.

A race is marked “not final” where the leader was ahead by fewer than six percentage points in that count.

New members of Congress take office Jan. 3, 2027. State offices change hands Jan. 1, 2027.

The idea for this directory, who belongs in it and how it should look came from the author (a human). The code that gathered and assembled it was written by Anthropic’s Claude and Claude Code, under close direction. The quality checks: every roster was read from the government body that publishes it; every address and phone number typed in by hand was checked by script against a saved copy of the page it came from, and the build stops if one is missing; every staff contact found by web search was re-downloaded from its source and matched character for character before it was allowed in; and election winners were taken from the Board of Elections’ own count. The risks of using AI remain. Staff rosters go stale quickly. Please flag anything that looks wrong.
