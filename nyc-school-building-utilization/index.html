The empty classroom and the crowded one

Across 1,350 New York City public school buildings with reported target capacity in 2025–26, 301 sit below 60% full while 337 run above 100% — including 109 overcrowded buildings within half a mile of an underused one and 46 shared buildings where a school over 100% capacity and a school below 60% operate under the same roof.

Since the 2015–16 school year, total enrollment across buildings tracked in the annual Enrollment, Capacity and Utilization Report (known as the Blue Book), published jointly by the New York City School Construction Authority (SCA) and Department of Education (DOE), has fallen by 172,783 students, from 1,035,617 to 862,834, while target building capacity changed by less than 1% (from 1,082,219 seats to 1,076,362). Citywide building utilization now stands at 80.2%, leaving 213,528 net empty target seats. Yet those empty seats are not where the crowded classrooms are: every one of the city’s 32 community school districts contains both a building below 60% of target capacity and a building above 100%, and when building utilization is paired with the DOE’s Nov. 2025 class-size compliance report under New York State Education Law 211-d, only 52.1% of non-exempt classes in overcrowded buildings meet the state caps, compared with 80.1% in buildings below 60% full.

2. Where the state class-size mandate collides with building utilization

New York State Education Law 211-d requires the DOE to phase in hard caps on K–12 class sizes — 20 students in grades K–3, 23 in grades 4–8 and 25 in high school — across all non-exempt classes by 2027–28. Matching the DOE’s Nov. 15, 2025 school-level compliance report (covering 120,035 non-exempt classes and 22,527 exempted classes as of Oct. 31, 2025) to the buildings where those schools sit shows that class-size compliance tracks building utilization directly.

In the 301 buildings operating below 60% of target capacity, 80.1% of non-exempt classes (20,268 of 25,301) already meet the state caps, and the DOE claimed zero space exemptions. In the 337 buildings above 100% of target capacity, only 52.1% of non-exempt classes (19,448 of 37,317) meet the caps — and the DOE exempted another 9,209 classes across 79 of those overcrowded buildings on the grounds that physical classroom space does not exist in the building.

How this was done

Primary sources and record counts. Building enrollment, target capacity and target utilization come from the New York City School Construction Authority (SCA) and Department of Education (DOE) Enrollment, Capacity and Utilization Report (the Blue Book), Target Calculation, published on NYC Open Data (gkd7-3vk7). The multi-vintage archive contains 20,754 rows across eight Data As Of snapshots. One snapshot (02/28/2026) contains only 6 rows and is excluded; the remaining seven complete editions cover the 2013–14 through 2025–26 school years (matched by comparing edition enrollment totals against New York State Education Department Oct. 31 audited registers).

In the 2025–26 edition (Data As Of = 09/09/2026, reflecting the Oct. 31, 2025 audited register), there are 3,132 organization-by-building rows across 1,384 distinct Bldg ID codes. Each row repeats building-level totals (Bldg Enroll, Target Bldg Cap, Target Bldg Util) alongside organization-level figures (Org Enroll, Org Target Cap, Org Target Util) and an Incl. Class flag for District 75 special education inclusion programs housed inside host buildings. Deduplicating by Bldg ID and dropping 34 administrative or unrated building codes with zero or blank Target Bldg Cap yields 1,350 buildings (1,334 with positive student enrollment and 16 with zero enrollment in 2025–26).

Geocoding and proximity pairs. Building codes were matched to coordinates using the DOE Location Code Generation and Management System (LCGMS) school directory (fetched Oct. 7, 2026), the DOE 2019–20 School Locations dataset (wg9x-4ke6) and the Organization-ID-to-DBN crosswalk from the Blue Book, geocoding 1,344 of 1,350 buildings (99.6%). For each of the 337 geocoded buildings over 100% of target capacity, straight-line haversine distance was computed to every active building (enrollment > 0) below 60% of target capacity. Pairs that share any school organization (such as a school’s main building and its annex) or sit at identical campus coordinates (<0.01 miles) are excluded from the proximity count.

Co-location splits and state class-size compliance. A building is counted as a co-located split if it houses at least two active K–12 school organizations (matching pattern ^[MXKQR]\d{3}$, Incl. Class = "", with at least 50 enrolled students and 50 target seats, excluding Alternate Learning Centers and citywide Pathways to Graduation programs) where at least one organization exceeds 100% of its assigned target capacity and another is below 60%. Class-size compliance figures come from the DOE’s Nov. 15, 2025 Class Size Report (Table C, data as of Oct. 31, 2025) under New York State Education Law 211-d, joined by DBN to each school’s primary building.

Limitations. Blue Book target capacity reflects the SCA’s formula for standard K–12 class sizes, specialty rooms and cluster rooms; physical room reassignments between co-located schools require Community Education Council review under New York State Education Law 2590-h. Proximity distances between buildings are straight-line miles and do not account for grade-span mismatches (for example, an overcrowded elementary school near an underused high school campus) or highway barriers, though building names and housed organizations are shown for every pair so readers can inspect grade compatibility directly.
