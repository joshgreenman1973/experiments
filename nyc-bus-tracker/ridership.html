    Methodology

    Source: MTA Bus Stop Level Ridership (data.ny.gov, dataset fvdm-uavx, published June 2026), hourly boardings per stop, route and direction, derived from Automatic Passenger Counters on board MTA buses. Stop locations and names from MTA bus GTFS feeds (all five boroughs plus MTA Bus Company).

    What the numbers are: all of June 2026, summed per stop and hour across every route and direction, then divided by the number of days of that type — 22 weekdays or 8 weekend days — to give an average day. Juneteenth (Friday, June 19) ran holiday service but is counted as a weekday. Hours follow the MTA service day, so boardings between midnight and about 3 a.m. belong to the previous service date.

    Limits: passenger counters miss some riders and some buses lack them; the MTA treats these figures as estimates, and so should you. 298 stop IDs (0.63% of June boardings) could not be matched to a GTFS location and are excluded. Circle area is proportional to boardings in the selected hour.

    Map layers: subway lines are drawn from MTA subway GTFS shapes and stations from the MTA Subway Stations dataset. "Beyond the subway" highlights bus stops more than 800 meters (about a half mile) from the nearest subway station, measured straight-line; stops closer than that are dimmed, not hidden. The congestion zone outline is the MTA's official Central Business District geofence; the share shown counts boardings at stops inside any geofence polygon, which includes stops on the exempt highway ribbons.

    Aggregation code and this page's data file live in the project repo.
