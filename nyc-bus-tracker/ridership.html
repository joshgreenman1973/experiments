    Methodology

    Source: MTA Bus Stop Level Ridership (data.ny.gov, dataset fvdm-uavx, published June 2026), hourly boardings per stop, route and direction, derived from Automatic Passenger Counters on board MTA buses. Stop locations and names from MTA bus GTFS feeds (all five boroughs plus MTA Bus Company).

    What the numbers are: all of June 2026, summed per stop and hour across every route and direction, then divided by the number of days of that type — 22 weekdays or 8 weekend days — to give an average day. Juneteenth (Friday, June 19) ran holiday service but is counted as a weekday. Each row's hour almost always falls on its own calendar date; on a sample day 12 rows out of 260,109 carried the following midnight, and those fold into the midnight hour here.

    Limits: passenger counters miss some riders and some buses lack them; the MTA treats these figures as estimates, and so should you. 298 stop IDs (0.63% of June boardings) could not be matched to a GTFS location and are excluded. Circle area is proportional to boardings in the selected hour.

    Subway layer. Stations come from the MTA Subway Stations dataset, all 496 of them, each collapsed to a single point. That file includes the 21 Staten Island Railway stations, so the SIR counts as subway here. The lines are a reference basemap only: the two busiest shapes per route, every third vertex, so branches and yard tracks are missing.

    "Beyond the subway" highlights stops more than 800 meters — about a half mile — from the nearest station, measured straight-line, not walking distance; closer stops are dimmed rather than hidden. On that measure 38.3% of stops are beyond the threshold; excluding the Staten Island Railway would raise it to 41.5%. The percentage on screen is the share of boardings at those stops, which is lower than the share of stops because busy stops cluster near the subway.

    Congestion zone. The outline is the MTA's official Central Business District geofence. The law defines the zone as Manhattan south of and including 60th Street, excluding the FDR Drive, the West Side Highway/Route 9A, the Battery Park Underpass and the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel surface connections. The dataset ships seven unlabeled polygons; we take the largest (20.8 km²) as the district and the other six as those excluded roadways, so a stop counts as inside only if it falls in the district and in none of the exclusions. That inference is ours, not the MTA's labeling — it puts 959 stops inside the zone, against 983 if the roadway polygons were counted as part of it.

    The geography layers are rebuilt by scripts/build_geo_layers.py in the repo, which prints every figure quoted here.
