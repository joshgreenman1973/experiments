Riders

Average weekday boardings by month from the passenger counters on the buses, beside paid fare taps (OMNY and MetroCard). Both are partial: taps miss riders who don’t pay, and counters miss riders on buses without a working counter. If buses go fare-free, taps stop and the counters remain, so how the two track each other on this route is worth knowing now. A sudden change in the ratio usually means a counting change, not a change in riders.

How these numbers are made

Readings. A collector records every bus in the Metropolitan Transportation Authority’s BusTime feed about a dozen times in six minutes, twice an hour from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. Eastern. A bus seen in two consecutive snapshots gives one reading: straight-line distance over the time between its two GPS timestamps. Readings on layover, readings where the position did not refresh and readings of 60 mph or more are dropped.

Speed with stops is total distance over total time for the route in each hour, stopped time included. Moving speed averages only readings of 0.5 mph or more. Time stopped is the share of reading time under 0.5 mph.

Weekly figures average each Eastern clock hour across the week’s weekdays, then give every hour the route ran equal weight. A route’s week is comparable when the whole system’s collection caught every hour on at least three weekdays (up to two missed hours are filled from the route’s usual shape of the day) and the route ran on at least three of them. Major holidays and weekdays when far fewer routes ran than usual are left out.

The MTA’s figure is the plain mean of the route’s weekday hourly speeds from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. in data.ny.gov r6db-kkzj, built the same way. It is published about six weeks after the month ends.
