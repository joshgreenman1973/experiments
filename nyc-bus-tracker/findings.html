      How these numbers are made

      Positions. A long-running GitHub Actions job asks the MTA BusTime API for the location of every bus in service about a dozen times over six minutes, at 9 and 39 minutes past every hour from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. Eastern. Raw snapshots are archived on the repo’s bus-data branch; nothing here is from a model or a guess about where buses were.

      Speed with stops. A bus seen in two consecutive snapshots gives one reading: the straight-line distance between its two GPS positions over the time between its two GPS timestamps. Readings from buses on layover at the end of the line, readings where the position did not refresh and readings of 60 mph or more are dropped. For each route and Eastern clock hour, speed is total distance over total time, so time stopped at stops and lights counts. Moving speed, the measure this page used until October 2026, averages only readings of 0.5 mph or more and reads about a mile an hour faster.

      Weighting. Local, limited and Select Bus Service routes each count once; express routes and temporary shuttles are left out of the system figures. Each hour from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. counts equally. Weekdays and weekends are averaged separately. Major holidays, and weekdays when far fewer routes ran than usual, are left out of the weekday figures.

      Comparable weeks. Collection misses some bursts, and which hours get missed varies. A week counts only when all seven days are in and every hour of the day was caught on at least three weekdays and on at least one weekend day. Up to two hours a week may be missed for each day type; a missed hour is assumed to have moved like the rest of that week’s day relative to its usual level, which leaves a fully caught week’s figure exactly as it is. The 8 p.m. hour on weekends was often missed before October 2026. Before late June 2026 the collector caught only some hours of most days, so few spring weeks qualify.

      Buses on the road counts buses in the feed not on layover. Trips scheduled comes from the MTA’s published timetable (GTFS) from Sept. 6, 2026: trips under way at each hour. A trip not run, or a bus whose GPS isn’t reporting, puts buses on the road below it.

      No wait or bunching figures. An audit in September 2026 found the earlier estimates could not be defended. They have been removed while a replacement wait measure is built.

      Riders come from the MTA’s Bus Stop Level Ridership dataset, boardings recorded by automatic passenger counters, beside paid OMNY and MetroCard taps from the MTA’s hourly ridership datasets. Weekday averages divide Monday-to-Friday boardings by the number of calendar weekdays. Months missing any days are dropped.

      What we’ve learned is generated from the same file the charts use. “The first weeks” and “recently” are the first and last four comparable weeks. The trend is a least-squares line through every comparable week.

      The MTA check. Each week a second job pulls the MTA’s own published bus speeds from data.ny.gov and builds them the same way: for each day type and hour, the plain mean of local, limited and Select Bus Service route speeds, then the mean over hours, weekdays and weekends blended 5 to 2. The forecast of the MTA’s next headline figure applies the tracker’s own month-to-month change to the MTA’s last published month; every forecast is recorded before the MTA publishes and scored after. Script: scripts/fetch_mta_official.py; results in official.json.

      Records and seasons. The monthly line is the MTA’s own published speed (data.ny.gov cudb-vcni), every route weighted by the miles it ran, back to January 2019. The August-to-September panel shows each year’s change in the MTA’s series, with the tracker’s own change for the current year alongside; compare the changes, not the levels.

      Method version 2. In October 2026 every day since April 22, 2026, was reprocessed from the raw snapshots with the method above, so the whole series is computed one way. The method changelog and data dictionary are in the download folder. Code: project repo.

      The concept, the choice of data and the layout of this page came from its author, a human. The code was written mainly by Anthropic's Claude, under close direction. The checks included recomputing the headline figures directly from the published weekly files and checking them against MTA datasets. Speeds are estimates made from GPS positions, not official MTA numbers, and AI-built pipelines can still go wrong. Please flag anything that looks off.
