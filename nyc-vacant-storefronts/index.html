NYC vacant storefronts: six years of the registry

Every storefront New York City property owners reported vacant from 2020 through 2025. Because owners re-file year after year, this data shows not just where storefronts sit empty but how long — the persistence a single survey can only estimate.

Built on: the New York City Department of Finance's “Storefronts Reported Vacant or Not” registry on NYC Open Data — the public database every covered property owner must file under Local Law 157 of 2019. Inspired by the New York City Comptroller's April 2026 report “Who's Minding the Storefronts?” This tool uses a different, public dataset to extend that analysis through time; see how the two compare →

How this differs from the comptroller's report. The comptroller used a private vendor's single April 2026 snapshot to estimate an 11% citywide vacancy rate. This tool uses the city's own public registry — self-reported, incomplete, but six years deep — so it can follow the same address through time and show genuine chronic vacancy rather than a one-day photograph.

Methodology, data caveats and how the numbers are built

Data: NYC Department of Finance, Storefronts Reported Vacant or Not (registry under Local Law 157 of 2019), reporting periods ending 2020–2025. The registry is owner-self-reported and does not cover every storefront, so figures here describe registered storefronts, not a true citywide census.  |  Methodology  |  Comparison report: NYC Comptroller, "Who's Minding the Storefronts?"
