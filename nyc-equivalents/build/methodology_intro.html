What this is

Each line on the graphic takes one figure about New York City and finds a place or thing somewhere else whose comparable figure comes in at about the same number. The point is scale, so pairs are chosen for closeness first and familiarity second.

Two kinds of comparison, and why the mix matters

70 of the 83 lines compare the same measure on both sides: subway riders against another city's subway riders, street trees against another city's street trees, Medicaid enrollment against a state's Medicaid enrollment, fire hydrants against another city's hydrants, dollars against dollars. These are the informative pairings, because both numbers were produced to measure the same thing.

The remaining 13 compare a New York City count against a population or a length, which carries no information about the measure and works only as a sense of magnitude. Every one of them is there because a same-measure comparison was searched for and missed. Three are deliberate distance comparisons rather than populations: sidewalks against the Great Wall, sewers against the width of the Earth, bike lanes against the Colorado River.

What was checked and missed, so the failures are on the record. No other American city's shelter system approaches New York City's: even California's entire sheltered homeless count, 66,351, falls 21 percent short, and Massachusetts, with a right to shelter, has 26,563. No state's cash-assistance caseload fits, because New York City's figure blends federal and state-only programmes that federal data does not count together. No park anywhere draws Central Park's 42 million: the National Mall, at about 32 million, is the closest. No city's 311 system comes near New York City's 3.66 million requests; Chicago, the closest, is 46 percent below. Madrid's buses carry 512 million riders against New York City Transit's 442 million, 16 percent too many. Ireland has the most street lighting of anywhere checked at 480,000 against nearly 400,000, still 20 percent off. London licenses 105,607 private-hire drivers against New York City's 180,334. New South Wales ambulances answered 1.4 million calls against the Fire Department's 1.62 million incidents, and the two count differently. Barcelona's international visitor total had to be derived from two separate figures rather than read off a page, so it was not used.

How the pairs were made

Every number on both sides had to come from a page that was actually fetched during the research, and the exact words on that page containing the number are quoted below. Numbers that could not be fetched with a verbatim quote were left out, even when they were widely reported. Primary sources were preferred: the Census Bureau, city agencies and the Mayor's Management Report, transit operators, the city and state comptrollers, the International Monetary Fund's World Economic Outlook database for national GDP, the World Bank's population interface and national statistics offices.

The ratio on each line is the comparison figure divided by the New York City figure. The bar on the graphic draws New York City at full width and the comparison at that ratio. Gaps are shown to one decimal place under 2 percent and rounded otherwise.

What does not line up

Years differ on some lines because agencies publish on different schedules; each entry gives the year on both sides. National GDP figures for 2025 are IMF estimates in nominal dollars at market exchange rates, which move with currencies, and borough economies are pinned to 2024 because county figures lag about two years. Some city figures are rounded on the source page and the ratio is taken against the round number. Definitions are noted where they differ: filled jobs rather than authorised posts, lane miles rather than centreline miles, homicides rather than murders, a fiscal-year average rather than a point-in-time count, a metropolitan area or a county rather than a city.

How to check it

Three Census Bureau links need a free API key to return data; the same tables are readable without one on data.census.gov and Census Reporter. The International Monetary Fund link serves one large file of every country; search it for the three-letter country code. Follow the two links in any entry and search the page for the quoted words. The map pins each comparison at its capital, its city, or the thing itself. Country outlines are Natural Earth 1:110m via the world-atlas package.
