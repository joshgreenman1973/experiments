Methodology

How each pairing was built, where both numbers come from, the exact words on each source page and what does not line up perfectly.

What this is

Each line on the graphic takes one figure about New York City and finds a place or thing somewhere else whose comparable figure comes in at about the same number. The point is scale, so the pairs are chosen for closeness first and familiarity second. A few pairs were passed over when the only close match was obscure, and one was kept at an 11 percent gap because the editor asked for it: New York City's murders against a smaller American city.

How the pairs were made

Every number on both sides had to come from a page that was actually fetched during the research, and the exact words on that page containing the number are quoted below. Numbers that could not be fetched with a verbatim quote were left out, even when they were widely reported. Primary sources were preferred: the Census Bureau, city agencies and the Mayor's Management Report, the MTA, the city and state comptrollers, the International Monetary Fund's World Economic Outlook database (for national GDP), the World Bank's population API and national statistics offices. Where the only fetchable source was a news report quoting an agency, that is said in the entry.

The ratio on each line is the comparison figure divided by the New York City figure. The bar on the graphic draws New York City at full width and the comparison at that ratio. Gaps are shown to one decimal place under 2 percent and rounded otherwise.

What does not line up

Years differ on some lines because agencies publish on different schedules; each entry gives the year on both sides. National GDP figures for 2025 are IMF estimates in nominal dollars at market exchange rates, which move with currencies. Some city figures are rounded on the source page ("nearly 400,000 streetlights," "over 800 bridges and tunnels," "more than 30,000 acres") and the ratio is taken against the round number. Definitions are noted where they differ: authorized positions rather than employees, lane miles rather than centerline miles, homicides rather than murders, a fiscal-year average rather than a point-in-time count.

How to check it

Follow the two links in any entry and search the page for the quoted words. The map pins each comparison at its capital, its city, or the thing itself; a straight-line distance is pinned at the far end of the line. Country outlines are Natural Earth 1:110m via the world-atlas package.
