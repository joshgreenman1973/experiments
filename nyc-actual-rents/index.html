What New Yorkers Actually Pay in Rent

The median asking rent on StreetEasy was $4,150 in September 2026. But more than half the city’s rental housing is regulated, and the median rent New Yorkers paid in 2023 was $1,641.

Median

The exact midpoint of all rents paid. Half of households pay less, half pay more. Extremes don’t pull it up or down, which makes it the better single number for a typical rent.

Mean (Average)

Adds every dollar paid across all units and divides by the count. A handful of $15,000-a-month apartments in Manhattan can drag this number far above what most people experience.

By Housing Type
The rent spectrum: from public housing to asking price

New York’s rental market is several markets layered on top of each other. The bars below show the median rent for each major housing type, from NYCHA public housing to what landlords ask for unregulated vacancies.

52% of New York City’s rental units are regulated—rent-stabilized, rent-controlled, public housing or in other programs such as Mitchell-Lama (about 2%, not shown). Asking rents on listing sites come only from the roughly 48% of units rented at market rates, and only from those vacant at the time.

By Unit Size
Median rent by number of bedrooms

Median rents change little from studios to two-bedrooms and rise at three bedrooms. At every size, the market-rate median runs $445 to $700 above the rent-stabilized one. A rent-stabilized two-bedroom ($1,505) costs about $500 less than a market-rate studio ($2,001).

Methodology & Sources
Where this data comes from

Every number on this page except the $4,150 asking rent is drawn from official government surveys and administrative records. That one figure comes from StreetEasy’s September 2026 report, three years after the 2023 survey, and listing sites capture only vacant, unregulated units seeking tenants. The key sources:

  • NYC Housing and Vacancy Survey (NYCHVS), 2023
    Mandated by state and city law, conducted by the U.S. Census Bureau. Covers a representative sample of all 2.3 million occupied rental units in New York City, including rent-stabilized, rent-controlled and public housing. Selected Initial Findings (PDF)
  • NYC Rent Guidelines Board
    Publishes annual reports on rent-stabilized housing, including the Income and Affordability Study and the Income and Expense Study. The RGB sets annual rent adjustment percentages for stabilized leases. Research portal
  • NYCHA Fact Sheet, 2024
    Administrative data on New York City Housing Authority residents: average family rent ($621/month), average household income ($26,129/year), and unit counts across 335 developments. NYCHA Fact Sheet (PDF)
  • American Community Survey (ACS), U.S. Census Bureau
    Annual survey providing rent and income data at the census tract level. Distinguishes between contract rent and gross rent (which includes utilities). ACS 2023, Table B25064
  • NYC Comptroller’s Office
    Published a 2023 spotlight report explaining the structural gap between asking rents and actual rents paid, citing the role of rent regulation in anchoring the market. Spotlight: NYC’s Rental Housing Market
